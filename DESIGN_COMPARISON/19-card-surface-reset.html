The four decisions

  • KeptCard stays neutral. No hue on the pane — the badge is still the one colour move. The 2026-08-20 ruling stands.
  • ReversalThe ⤢ is back, between your controls and the fold. Reverses 2026-08-22 (“user just presses the title”).
  • ReversalRows are boxed again. Reverses 2026-08-15 flush rows and reopens open conflict #8.
  • NewDepth is chosen by the card, not the user — quick row, sheet, or full editor.

Why it looked clouded

A card’s fill was doing nothing. In dark, bg #000000 against surface #1E1E1E is 1.26:1 — so the only thing separating a card from the page was a 1px edge that faded to transparent on its bottom-right by design.

Move the fill and the card becomes an object again. Find the value you want:

surface#2C2C2C
vs bg1.50:1
text on it14.0:1

Shipped is #1E1E1E (1.26:1). This opens at #2C2C2C (1.50:1). The real ceiling is rule #3A3A42 — push the surface up to meet it and the row dividers vanish, so the top of this slider is where the ladder runs out, not where taste does.

It also helps the halation ceiling that rule 10a defends: white text goes 16.7:1 → 14.0:1, further inside the bound rather than past it.

The glow budget

The mockup’s own rule, now enforced: text, borders and backgrounds never glow. Press Show what’s lit to count the light sources on any screen.

  • LitThe badge glyph
  • LitAn active check, a filled bar or ring
  • LitThe primary key, and a field while focused
  • DarkThe card edge — a border, not a light source
  • DarkA resting composer. This was the loudest thing on every screen.
  • DarkThe backdrop. Orbs held at 12–13%.

Two things building this turned up

1. The ⤢ costs title width, and here is the bill. Swept all five screens × three widths × both languages, with the buttons already trimmed to the app’s own draw-36 / touch-48:

⤢ in the header6 of 30 truncate
⤢ off, title opens1 of 30 truncate

All of it is at 360px; 393 and 430 are clean either way. The pattern is exact: any card carrying its own control — Catalogue’s lock, Medicine’s bell — plus the ⤢ plus the fold. Three buttons is one too many at that width.

The toggle in the bar switches between them, live. I would keep your ruling and take the ⤢, then drop the fold chevron on cards that carry a control — but that is a third option, and it is your call.

2. Closed cards all look the same. Open To-do and you get seven near-identical bars — which is your “no order, no logic” note again, in its purest form. The surface fix does not touch this. It is a resting-state question, and the honest answers are: fewer cards per screen, or let the first card on each screen rest open. Worth deciding separately.
